# Product Vision

**Status:** Review
**Version:** 0.1

## Vision

Help families turn an everyday hour into purposeful learning through hands-on, inventive, and personalized activities that primarily use common objects.

## Problem

Adults find many ideas for experiments and crafts, but they are often incomplete, repetitive, lack pedagogical explanation, or include unclear risks. It is also difficult to choose an activity suitable for multiple children, prepare materials, respond when something goes wrong, and understand what is developing over time.

## Primary user

The operational user is a responsible adult who prepares or accompanies activities with one or more children. The child is a beneficiary and participant, but the product must initially be designed as a tool for the adult.

## Market and initial format

- Market: United States.
- Launch languages: English and Spanish.
- Platform: mobile application for iOS and Android; editorial web portal and possible subsequent family web application.
- Initial children's audience: 5–10 years old.
- Commercial model: monthly or annual subscription paid by an adult.
- Commercial trial: seven days, separate from the free pilot.
- Account: a family can authorize multiple adults and create the child profiles they need; MVP optimizes a session for 1–4 children.

## Promise

The system will recommend a safe and appropriate activity, explain its purpose, take advantage of available materials, assign a meaningful challenge to each child, and learn from brief observations without making the adult the data manager.

## Differentiators

- Validated library, no unrestricted live generation.
- Personalization by evidence and experience, not just by age.
- The same activity can include several children with different contributions and objectives.
- Individual low-friction evaluation.
- Traceability: the system explains why it recommends or infers.
- Invention philosophy: **the world is the kit**.
- Contextual AI support during the activity.
- Mobile experience that preserves the downloaded weekly package to execute activities with limited connectivity.

## Desired results

### For the adult

- Spend less time searching, rebuilding and verifying instructions.
- Know why an activity is carried out and what to observe.
- Be able to facilitate learning for several children without running separate plans.
- Understand learning trends without receiving absolute labels.

### For the child

- Build, experiment, explain and improve.
- Develop autonomy, curiosity, persistence and concrete skills.
- Discover new uses for everyday materials.
- Receive challenges tailored to their experience and way of participating.

## Non-objectives

- Replace school, teachers, medical care or professional evaluation.
- Diagnose development, intelligence, disorders, or disabilities.
- Continuously monitor or record the child.
- Reward accumulation of screens or dependency on the application.
- Generate dangerous activities without review.
- Require proprietary kits for the system to have value.

## Product hypothesis

1. A small but proven library generates more trust than hundreds of generic activities.
2. A brief assessment of the primary objective produces sufficient signal to improve recommendations.
3. Differentiated contributions solve a real need for families with children of different ages.
4. AI contextual help reduces abandonment when an activity fails.
5. Personalization will be valuable if its inferences are explainable and correctable.

## Initial metrics

- Percentage of started activities that reach close-out.
- Real adult preparation time.
- Time to complete final evaluation.
- Percentage of plans made with materials already available.
- Frequency of “too easy” or “too difficult”.
- Perceived usefulness of AI help.
- Rate and nature of corrections to system inferences.
- Family retention without using addictive mechanisms aimed at the child.
