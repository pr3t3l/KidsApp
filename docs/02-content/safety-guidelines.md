# Activity Safety Guide

**Status:** Draft — requires expert review before commercial publication
**Version:** 0.1

## Beginning

Curiosity does not justify unnecessary risk. The product should prefer a safe alternative that teaches the same concept.

## Participation levels

| Level | Description |
|---|---|
| A | The child handles low-risk materials under normal supervision. |
| B | The child participates with close help; There are tools or parts that require control. |
| C | The adult performs specific steps and the child participates in others. |
| D | Exclusively adult demonstration; outside the basic pilot and subject to special review. |

These levels do not replace specific risks and controls.

## Risk categories

- Ingestion and toxicity.
- Small parts and choking.
- Cuts and perforations.
- Heat, flame and burns.
- Electricity.
- Pressure, projectiles and closed containers.
- Glass and breakage.
- Irritation of skin, eyes or breathing.
- Water, spills and slippery surfaces.
- Allergies and food contamination.
- Drawstrings, bags and risk of entrapment.

## Initial pilot restrictions

- No flammable liquids or burning alcohol.
- No reactions inside sealed rigid containers.
- No projectiles towards people or animals.
- Without connection to domestic current; only approved low voltage supplies.
- No improvised chemical heating.
- Without glass when there is a functional plastic alternative.
- No tasting except explicitly designed and reviewed food activity.
- Balloons, magnets, batteries, motors, scissors and tools require specific controls.

## Later special demonstrations

The founder wants to preserve the possibility of teaching concepts through flame, glass or pressure when the adult performs the operation. These activities are not automatically considered safe because they seem common. They will only be able to enter after the basic pilot when:

- There is a low-risk alternative and an explanation is given as to why it is not useful or does not teach the same thing.
- An appropriate specialist check materials, quantities, assembly and failure.
- All risk steps are exclusively for adults.
- The child maintains explicit distance and observational role.
- Surface, ventilation, protection, cooling and cleaning are defined.
- The content has reinforced editorial gate and cannot be adapted by AI.

The covered candle raising water experiment is tentatively classified as adult demonstration D due to flame, hot/breakable container, and risk of spillage. It will not be part of the initial pilot library.

## Authorship rules

- Identify danger, exposed person, condition and control.
- Assign each step to adult or child.
- Indicate preparation and cleaning.
- Define when to stop the activity.
- Test the failure safely.
- Do not allow AI to remove warnings or reassign an adult step.
- Remove a version when an unforeseen risk appears.

## AI Risks

- A seemingly innocuous substitution can change toxicity, pressure or resistance.
- An extension may introduce unevaluated energy or speed.
- The analysis of a photo does not replace direct supervision.
- The assistant must recognize uncertainty and recommend stopping when safety cannot be verified.

## Requirements

- **SAFE-001:** Every published activity has a registered safety review.
- **SAFE-002:** Each hazard has associated controls and steps.
- **SAFE-003:** Adult-only steps cannot be automatically reassigned.
- **SAFE-004:** Adaptations cannot increase energy, temperature, pressure, toxicity or speed outside approved limits.
- **SAFE-005:** The system must allow immediate retirement of a version.
- **SAFE-006:** Critical warnings appear before and during the relevant step.
- **SAFE-007:** The recommender filters by participants and declared restrictions.
- **SAFE-008:** Activities C/D require specialist gate and cannot be published with the standard A/B gate.
- **SAFE-009:** Materials that can produce toxic vapors, violent or explosive reactions are prohibited for family activities.
